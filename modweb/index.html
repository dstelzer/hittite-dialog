Ḫattušumna Laḫḫa (“Hittite Adventure”) is the provisional name given to a series of recently-discovered tablets in New Hittite script. The text describes an unidentified hero’s attempt to infiltrate an enemy city and recover a stolen god; while the historical context is unknown, the focus on rival kings kidnapping each others’ gods suggests a pre-Empire origin.

Three tablets have been uncovered so far:

  • In Tablet 1, the hero slips past the city walls under cover of night.
  • Tablet 2 describes the hero crafting a disguise, posing as a temple baker bringing fresh loaves of bread. It ends when he finally enters the temple proper.
  • A significant amount of text is missing between tablets 2 and 3; from context, it seems a royal retinue arrived before the hero could make his escape.
  • In Tablet 3, the hero uncovers a conspiracy to murder the enemy king, and uses that conspiracy to create a distraction, seize his god, and flee.

This is a game (actually a series of three short games) presented entirely in the ancient Hittite language. It’s meant for absolute beginners—no knowledge of Hittite is needed!

Start with tablet 1, which will introduce the basic grammar and vocabulary. After that, you can play either tablet 2 (which covers logograms and more noun cases, with classic text adventure puzzles) or tablet 3 (which covers quotations and subordinate clauses, with logical deduction puzzles).

If you’re completely new to Hittite, the default settings are a good starting point. Playing a game in a foreign language is hard enough already; there’s no need to make it any harder! But the options menu in the top right corner offers some ways to adjust the experience, if desired. You can turn off “double-click to insert”, for example, requiring you to type the Hittite words instead of just clicking them; this makes the game harder, but is better for actually learning the Hittite language. And advanced learners can switch the output from bound transcription to actual cuneiform, to practice reading the signs themselves. (Either way, though, you should type your input in bound transcription—typing cuneiform is currently not an easy feat.)

Have fun! When you’re done, check out the postscript.
