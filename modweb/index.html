Congratulations on finishing the game!

Have you learned the Hittite names for the compass directions? (Try looking at a nearby room.) These were only discovered in 2023! (Kreitzscheck, “The Hittite words for North, East, South, and West”)

There are two ways to deal with the baker in tablet 2, one violent, one non-violent. Both of them involve the door bolt from tablet 1.

The puzzle on tablet 3 is randomly generated every time you restart the game, with over ten thousand possibilities.

Hittite has a very simple noun gender system: all nouns are either “common” or “neuter”. This game doesn't put much focus on it, but the nouns that look the same in the nominative and accusative are neuter, and the ones that don't are common. (Neuter nouns in Hittite actually don't have a nominative or accusative at all, just a merged case called the “absolutive”.)

In other words...

This is a game (actually a series of three short games) presented entirely in the ancient Hittite language. It’s meant for absolute beginners—no knowledge of Hittite is needed!

Start with Tablet 1, which will introduce the basic grammar and vocabulary. After that, you can play either Tablet 2 (which covers logograms and more noun cases, with classic text adventure puzzles) or Tablet 3 (which covers quotations and subordinate clauses, with logical deduction puzzles).

Have fun! When you're done, check out the postscript.

Tip!

If you’re new to Hittite, the default settings are a good starting point—no need to make it harder! But the options menu in the top right (≡) offers some adjustments for advanced learners:

  • Turn off “double-click to insert” to force yourself to type Hittite commands (harder, but helps you actually learn the language).
  • Switch the output to actual cuneiform to practice reading the signs yourself. (You should still type your input in bound transcription, though—typing cuneiform is currently not an easy feat.)

Content warnings: Brief (and non-English) depictions of death and arson, with a typically Bronze Age disregard for human life. While the protagonist is never required to murder someone directly, their actions do result in death and destruction.
